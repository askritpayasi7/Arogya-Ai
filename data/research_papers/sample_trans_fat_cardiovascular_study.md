# Health Impacts of Industrially-Produced Trans-Fatty Acids on Cardiovascular Morbidity
Authors: Dr. R. Mozaffarian, Dr. A. Katan, Dr. M. Stampfer
Year: 2021
DOI: 10.1056/NEJMra054035
Journal: New England Journal of Medicine & Global Health Review

## Abstract and Introduction
Industrially produced trans-fatty acids (iTFAs), formed primarily during partial hydrogenation of edible vegetable oils, are non-essential dietary components associated with severe adverse metabolic consequences. This systematic review synthesizes clinical and epidemiological data on the physiological effects of TFA intake.

## Biochemical Mechanism and Lipid Profile Alteration
Consumption of trans-fatty acids produces a dual adverse effect on serum lipid biomarkers that is uniquely deleterious compared to any other dietary macronutrient:
1. Significant elevation of Low-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (LDL-C) and Apolipoprotein B.
2. Marked reduction in High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C) and Apolipoprotein A-1.
3. Increased systemic inflammation, as demonstrated by elevated high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels.
4. Impairment of vascular endothelial function, accelerating atherogenesis and coronary artery occlusion.

## Clinical Endpoints and Morbidity Risks
Meta-analyses of prospective cohort studies reveal that for every 2% increase in energy intake derived from trans-fatty acids, the relative risk of coronary heart disease (CHD) events increases by 23% (RR 1.23, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.37; p < 0.001). Furthermore, all-cause cardiovascular mortality rises significantly with habitual consumption of hydrogenated vegetable fats (vanaspati, shortening, margarine).

## Recommendations for Elimination
The World Health Organization (WHO) and clinical cardiology consensus bodies strongly advocate for the total elimination of industrially-produced trans fats from the global food supply chain, recommending that TFA intake not exceed 1% of total energy intake, or ideally 0 grams per serving.
